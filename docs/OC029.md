# OC-029 — lever-decomposition study: what actually saves money

**Status: executed. Four runs on OC-023's substrate decompose the savings
claim the review challenged — OC-012's 73% was measured against a
deliberately oversized baseline. Here the baseline is the case's own
competent pack (240×3, utilization 0.67 under the 0.8 gate), and each
lever is measured separately against it. The honest answer: sizing buys
nothing on this grid, grading buys ~12%, geometry buys ~28%, and real
product widths buy nothing at this tape count.**

## Substrate

OC-023's racetrack (0.9 T at the bore probe, bend 0.2 m, straight
half-length 0.3 m, 12 mm tape, SuperPower AP base at a declared synthetic
$30/m). Baseline 240×3 costs **$60,269.70** — a pack sized the way an
engineer would size it: meets the requirement with ~33% utilization
margin, not the 3× overbuild that made OC-012's headline misleading.

## Reading it

- **Sizing buys nothing** — the baseline is already the cheapest
  screening-passer at fixed geometry. Every smaller or narrower pack
  fails the utilization gate (300×2 hits 0.87 > 0.8). Optimizing turn
  count alone does not pay on a competent baseline.
- **Grading buys the pack sizing couldn't** — the premium outer-region
  tape drops the 300×2's limiting utilization to 0.80, letting a 600-turn
  pack pass where the uniform one fails. Grading's contribution is an
  *enabler*: it unlocks a topology, not a discount.
- **Geometry is the dominant lever** — a 0.15 m bend needs ~35% less
  conductor per turn than 0.2 m, and the whole 300×2 pack comes in at
  27.6% under baseline. Caveat: this substrate declares no bend-strain or
  mechanical bound, so the model prices a tighter magnet as pure
  conductor savings. A real study must bound the tight bend.
- **Vendor choice buys nothing here** — at their real widths (Shanghai
  4 mm, THEVA 6 mm) no declared tape count forms a viable pack; both rows
  FAIL honestly. Only the 12 mm product's width matches the case. A real
  procurement comparison would widen `tapes_along_width` per product —
  the grid, not the product, was the binding constraint.

## Levers are not additive

The table measures each lever *added to the same baseline*, in order —
not a full factorial. The geometry figure includes the sizing choice
(300×2); grading-on-top-of-geometry is unmeasured. Interactions are real:
premium tape at a tighter bend could unlock packs neither lever reaches
alone. A full decomposition needs the graded+axes run.

## Known limits

- All prices are declared synthetic placeholders; no procurement claim
  survives contact with a real quote.
- Two latent bugs surfaced and were fixed during this study: axis-case
  candidates were marked degenerate before the kernel (the v21 axis had
  never produced a PASS), and the acceptance cost recompute read
  unresolved dims (NaN on every axis run). Both fixed; the geometry
  record in `runs/oc-029-geometry.json` was regenerated under the fix.
- `runs/` artifacts: `oc-029-sizing.json`, `oc-029-geometry.json`,
  `oc-029-vendor-bakeoff.json`, `oc-023-walk.json` (the graded run).

## What this says about the ≥20% ambition

On a competent baseline, the honest modeled savings is ~28% — but
**entirely from geometry**, a lever that here carries no manufacturing
penalty. Grading alone gives 12%; sizing and vendor choice give 0%. A
commercially meaningful claim needs a bend-strain/mechanical bound on the
tight geometry, real prices, and the graded+geometry interaction run.
