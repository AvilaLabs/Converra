# CR-01 — Estimating tape performance from sparse measurements

Roadmap item: [CR-01](../ROADMAP.md#cr-01--estimate-tape-performance-from-sparse-measurements).
Status: **scored (attempt 01, 2026-10-04): FAIL in every scenario.**

## Question

Can a small subset of a REBCO tape's critical-current data, of the kind a reel-to-reel in-field scanner or a
short in-field sample provides, predict that tape's withheld critical current at 20–40 K and 5–8 T in
perpendicular field?

The answer determines what evidence a reel passport must contain before Converra can rely on it for allocation.

## Data

- **Source:** the public Robinson Research Institute critical-current database, figshare collection 2861821, CC BY 4.0.
- **Coverage:** 20 REBCO tape products, plus one YBCO film on sapphire that is reported separately, not in the
  headline.
- **Preparation:** downloaded workbooks were converted by `tools/cr01_prepare_robinson.py` into Converra's
  measurement columns. The originals and prepared files are kept locally under ignored
  `.local/research/cr01/robinson/`. Their identities are recorded in `sources.json` there.
- **Conversion check:** for the four products already embedded in `data/materials`, every overlapping row
  matched the embedded data exactly.

| Scenario | Inputs used for prediction | Expectation declared before the run |
| --- | --- | --- |
| S0 — 77 K self-field (control) | Self-field Ic at 77.5 K and a leave-one-product-out population lift factor | FAIL |
| S1 — scanner | Perpendicular-field data at 65–77.5 K, 0.5–4 T | Uncertain |
| S2 — scanner plus anchor | S1 plus one perpendicular point at 30 K, 3 T | Better than S1 |

**Model.** S1 and S2 use the three-parameter scaling `ln Ic = c0 − T/T* − α ln B` (Senatore et al. 2016),
fitted by least squares with a 95% prediction interval. A four-parameter variant is reported for diagnosis
only and cannot change a verdict.

**Product verdict.** A product PASSES when it meets both limits:

- worst overprediction ≤ 10%;
- 95th-percentile absolute error ≤ 20%.

Overprediction is limited more tightly because overpredicted capacity is unsafe in allocation.

**Scenario verdict.** A scenario PASSES when at least 80% of evaluated tape products pass. Per-product
verdicts are the primary result. Products missing required inputs are NOT_EVALUATED, never dropped.

**Evidence class.** Registered retrospective comparison. Earlier Converra studies used the 20–40 K data of the
four already-embedded products, so those four are also reported as a separate stratum.

any scenario.** Every software gate passed, so the failure belongs to the method, not the code.

### What the numbers show

**S0 (control) failed as declared.** The worst overprediction ranges from −0.76 (`superpower-hm`) to +4.52
(`sunam-san04200`). Overprediction exceeds 10% for 14 of 20 tape products. Two products come close on one
limit but fail the other:
- `sti-conductus`: worst overprediction +0.16, p95 0.15;
- `samri`: worst overprediction +0.02, p95 0.25.

This supports the premise that a 77 K self-field rating does not set the in-field operating current. The
falsifier for that premise did not fire.

**S1 overpredicts catastrophically.** Every product overpredicts every target. The worst overprediction ranges
from 2.2× actual (`sunam-han04200`, e = +1.24) to 167× actual (`superpower-hm`, e = +166). The 95% interval
covers no target for any product.

**S2 is better than S1, as declared, but still fails.**
- The worst overprediction ranges from +0.50 (`sti-conductus`) to +4.33 (`superpower-hm`).
- p95 errors range from 0.44 to 4.0.
- Interval coverage is 0.67–1.0, but only because the intervals are very wide.

**Four-parameter variant (M2, reported only).**
- S1: worse than M1.
- S2: overprediction vanishes for 18 of 19 products (worst e between −0.10 and −0.59; `sunam-san04200` +0.45).
  Errors are still too large: p95 is 0.40–0.83, above the 20% limit.

### Why S1 and S2 fail

The node values are physically plausible. For example, `superpower-advanced-pinning` gives 118.8 kA/m at
20 K / 5 T and 47.3 kA/m at 65 K / 0.5 T. The conversion matched the embedded data exactly (see Data). The
failure comes from the model and the training range:

1. **The 65–77.5 K scanner window lies close to Tc**, where Ic collapses steeply. Fitted there, the single
   exponential gives a short temperature scale: T* = 6.1–11.5 K across products. Extrapolated 25–57 K
   downward, exp(−T/T*) grows by orders of magnitude more than real tapes do.
2. **One anchor cannot correct the shape.**
   - In S2 the 30 K / 3 T anchor raises T* to 10–16 K. It is still one point among 25 in an unweighted fit.
   - The fitted S2 model overpredicts the anchor node itself by +30% to +136%.
   - A single exponential in T cannot pass through both the near-Tc data and the 30 K point.

   This is consistent with the scaling law's stated validity range (Senatore et al. 2016): one exponential
   describes Ic over a limited temperature span, not from near Tc down to 20 K.

### What this means for the reel passport

- **A 65–77 K scanner record alone, read through this model, cannot rate a tape for 20–40 K service.** It
  overpredicts unsafely for every product tested. Whether scanner data still ranks reels within one product
  is a separate question that this protocol did not test.
- **Adding one low-temperature point to an unweighted fit is not enough.** A better method is a new protocol
  version scored as a new attempt. Protocol v1, its tolerances and this result stay as recorded.
