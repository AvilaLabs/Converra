# CR-03 — Allocation and orientation

Roadmap item: [CR-03](../ROADMAP.md#cr-03--allocation-and-orientation).
Status: **slices A (demand table) and B (allocation, baseline, independent acceptance) implemented 2026-10-04;
slice C next.**

## Purpose

Assign the reels of an inventory to the positions of a fixed coil design. Every sampled position must then meet
its declared limit with the reel actually placed there. Derive the splice schedule. Report what this saves
against buying and screening to one uniform worst-case specification. Also say which reels are worth a
low-temperature measurement, because it would change the allocation.

## The key simplification

A reel's capacity relative to its product map is a scale factor s(x) along its length (CR-02 rule 7):

    s(x) = ic_a(x) / (map value at the profile condition × width)

It does not depend on the operating point. The engine's screening at a position allows

    allowed = (1 − budget) × width × K_map(T, B_local, θ_local) × strands

and passes when I_op ≤ u × allowed. A reel with scale s placed there passes when

    s ≥ s_req = I_op / (u × allowed_map)

So allocation compares two tables: the reels' s(x) and the coil's s_req at each position.

## Scope limits of v1

Each limit is stated in every allocation record.

- **One product map.** Every reel in the inventory must name the same product map. The coil's demand is
  computed with that map at every turn, regardless of the case's grading. Mixed-product allocation is later
  work.
- **Radial tape normal, legacy racetrack or path geometry.** These are the geometries `turn_ledger_rows`
  supports. Other geometries are refused with a reason.
- **Width.** Reel `width_m` must equal the case's tape width within 1e-9 m.
- **Streams.** Each (module, strand) is an independent conductor stream running through turns 1…n in winding
  order. Module joints are structural and counted. Pieces within a stream are joined by splices.
- **Sampling basis.** A turn's requirement is the maximum over the case's declared stations and width
  points. Positions between stations are not sampled, as in the search's own screening.
- **Orientation.**
  - The engine takes the minimum over each mirror pair of angles. Under that policy Ic at θ + o and at
    θ − o is identical near the tape plane, so choosing which face of a reel points inward cannot gain
    anything. v1 makes no flip choice and says so.
  - ab-plane offsets are applied conservatively: a reel's requirement is evaluated over the angle interval
    [θ − o, θ + o], where o is the largest |offset| + uncertainty on the reel.
  - A reel with no ab offsets cannot be placed at a position whose angle lies within 15° of the tape plane
    (as CR-02).

## Slice A — demand table, `optcoil-allocation-demand/v1`

**Input.** A coupled-search run record (exact bytes, SHA-256), the product map (dataset bundle or embedded),
and `angle_offsets_deg`: the offset bounds to tabulate. The default is 0 to 6° in 0.5° steps. A reel's bound is
rounded up to the next tabulated step. A reel whose bound exceeds the largest step cannot be placed at
offset-sensitive positions.

**Computation.**
1. Take the record's selected optimum (`candidates[best_index]`, which must have screening status PASS) and
   its embedded case.
2. Override every turn's material with the product map. Refuse if the product map's identity differs from the
   material the record screened with, unless `--allow-map-substitution` is given; the substitution is then
   recorded.
3. Build coupled cases with `build_coupled_case` for all turns 1…n, all modules 1…p and the case's stations, in
   chunks within the sampling caps. Run them with `run_coupled_case_with_datasets`.
4. For each width point, keep:
   - `query_field_t`, `angle_folded_deg`, `mirror_angle_deg` and `basis`;
   - the engine's `allowed_screening_a` at the tape level, and
   - I_op.
5. For each tabulated offset δ > 0, recompute each width point's K over the angle interval [θ − δ, θ + δ]. Use
   the engine's own query function (`query_mirror_pair_with_clamp`, same clamp and method), evaluated at
   θ − δ, θ and θ + δ and at every integer degree between them. Take the minimum. Recompute tape-level
   allowed exactly as the engine does from width points.
   - **Gate.** At δ = 0 the recomputation must equal the engine's `allowed_screening_a` within 1e-12 relative,
     for every tape. Any mismatch fails the demand build.
6. s_req(turn k, module j, δ) is the maximum over stations of I_op / (u × allowed). A position with any
   Unsupported basis is `outside_map_domain`, with no requirement value, and blocks allocation there with an
   explanation.
7. Turn lengths come from the engine's `turn_ledger_rows` walk, via a new `pub` wrapper. Stream coordinate ℓ
   runs from 0 at the start of turn 1 to the stream length.

**Output.** The record contains:
- identities: the record's SHA-256, the case's SHA-256, the product-map identity, and whether the map was
  substituted;
- geometry: n, p, strands and tape width;
- `utilization_limit` and `budget`;
- per turn: length, start ℓ, and per module s_req at each δ, plus flags for offset sensitivity (|θ − 90°| < 15
  at any point) and domain status;
- limitations.

**CLI.** `optcoil allocation demand RECORD [--product-map DATASET_ID:CSV_SHA256] [--dataset-bundle …] [--allow-map-substitution] [--angle-offsets-deg LIST] --output PATH`

**Implementation decisions recorded at slice A.**
- **Product map.** `--product-map` names the product map. It defaults to the record's base material, so
  substitution is detected against each turn's screened material.
- **Nested angle samples.** Each tabulated δ also includes the angle samples of every smaller δ. Without
  nesting, s_req could fall by about 3e-6 relative between steps. Nesting makes it monotone and more
  conservative.
- **Tape-level allowed.** It is (1 − budget) × width × the minimum K over the five Lobatto width points ×
  strands, as the engine computes it. The δ = 0 gate on the 70-turn, 2-module test fixture was bit-exact
  across 140 tapes.
- **Along-current points.** Positions blocked by an along-current exclusion get their own status,
  `along_current_excluded`, separate from `outside_map_domain`.
- **Angle folding (fixed after slice A was published).** Offset samples θ ± δ are folded into [0°, 180°) with
  the engine's own `theta_fold_deg` before querying. The first version passed angles below 0° unfolded. The
  result was conservative: positions where the field crosses the tape normal were wrongly blocked for δ ≥ 1°.
  On the 70-turn fixture the only position still blocked is turn 32 at δ ≥ 5°. Its mirror sample falls in a
  real 0.03° gap between the map's nominal-0° rows (measured at −0.64°) and its nominal-180° rows (measured at
  179.33°). The engine would refuse that point as well.
- **Unsampled turns.** Demand covers all n turns, while the search sampled only some. A PASS optimum can
  therefore show s_req > 1 at an unsampled turn, which the allocation will report.

## Slice B — allocation, baseline and independent acceptance, `optcoil-allocation/v1`

**Inputs.**
- The demand table and the inventory, both bound by SHA-256.
- Parameters, all declared and recorded:
  - `margin` ≥ 0: placement requires s_used ≥ s_req × (1 + margin);
  - `transfer_derate`: `{sigma, z}`. Each reel's s is multiplied by exp(−z σ). It is required, with no default.
    The published within-product spread is 0.13–0.20 at 20 K (CR-02). Choosing σ = 0 must be explicit and is
    labelled unsafe in the record;
  - `min_piece_length_m`;
  - `price_usd_per_m` with its source label.

**Reel preparation.**
- Usable sub-reels are the reel minus its `excluded` and `cut` spans. `accepted` spans stay in, with their
  profile values.
- Between profile points, capacity is the smaller endpoint value. This is conservative.
- A reel must have a CR-02 scaled rating basis, a profile that can be scaled with its `map_reference` or the
  interpolator; otherwise it is unallocatable, with the CR-02 explanation.

**Feasibility of a piece.** A piece from a sub-reel at offset a, of length λ, placed at stream offset ℓ₀, is
feasible when, for every u in [0, λ]:

    s_used(a + u) ≥ s_req(turn at ℓ₀ + u, module, δ_reel) × (1 + margin)

It is evaluated exactly on the merged breakpoints of both step functions. At offset-sensitive turns,
reels without ab offsets are infeasible.

**Greedy allocator (v1, deterministic, no optimality claim).**
1. Order streams by descending peak s_req.
2. Fill each stream from ℓ = 0.
3. At each head, consider every sub-reel at its current head. Its feasible run is the longest feasible λ, up
   to the remaining stream length and the remaining sub-reel length.
4. Among sub-reels whose run is at least min(remaining stream, `min_piece_length_m`), choose the smallest mean
   surplus (s_used / s_req) over the run, then the longest run, then the lowest reel id. If none qualifies,
   choose the longest run.
5. If every run has length 0, the stream is `infeasible_at`. Report the position (turn, ℓ), s_req, and the
   best s_used available.
6. A used sub-reel's head advances. Its tail stays available.

**Baseline: uniform worst-case specification.**
- A reel is accepted only if the minimum s_used over its usable length is at least
  max s_req × (1 + margin), over the whole coil, at that reel's δ.
- Rejected reels count as scrap.
- Accepted reels fill streams in order, with the same piece rules and no position dependence.
- The shortfall is the conductor that would have to be bought.

**Report.**
- Per stream: the splice schedule, as pieces in order with `reel_id`, reel from/to, stream from/to, turn
  from/to, and the minimum margin ratio within the piece.
- Counts: splices and pieces.
- Reels: used, partly used, unused but eligible, unallocatable (with reasons), and scrapped under the baseline
  but used here.
- Metres: allocated, remnant, and shortfall in both allocation and baseline.
- Money: the difference in money between allocation and baseline, at the declared price and labelled with
  the price source.
- Evidence class: synthetic if any input is synthetic; otherwise model_informed. Allocation rests on scaled
  ratings.

**Independent acceptance.** A separate module that does not call the allocator recomputes, from the demand
table, the inventory and the allocation record:
- every piece's feasibility;
- coverage: streams fully covered, with no overlaps;
- reel-length conservation: no reel segment used twice, and nothing inside excluded or cut spans;
- the splice counts and all accounting totals.

PASS requires exact agreement on integers and verdicts. Floats must agree within 1e-9 relative, with an absolute
floor of 1e-9 for values near zero.

**CLI.** `optcoil allocation run DEMAND INVENTORY --params PARAMS --output PATH` and
`optcoil allocation check DEMAND INVENTORY ALLOCATION`.

## Later slices

- **C — truth evaluation and measurement planning.**
  - With a synthetic truth file, evaluate an allocation against the true capacity s(x) × transfer factor. Report
    the length and positions where the truth falls below the requirement.
  - For each reel, report the effect of removing its derate, as if a low-temperature measurement were made, on
    shortfall, scrap and splices. Rank the reels.
- **D — re-allocation after a defect during winding.** Freeze the wound part, record the defect, re-allocate
  the rest, and chain records by SHA-256.
- **E — Python and MCP bindings.**
