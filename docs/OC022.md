# OC-022 — two-dataset graded pack: SuperPower AP + Shanghai SC HFLT

**Status: implemented; first graded benchmark over two physically distinct
measured datasets. `search_status PASS`, `acceptance agreement PASS`.**

## Why

OC-020 proved the grading machinery end-to-end, but its second tape spec
bound `robinson-superpower-ap-v3-lowfield` — a binned variant of the same
specimen. That demonstrates assignment plumbing, not a material-selection
decision: a graded design on one vendor's data can't show that a *different*
conductor wins where its measured response differs.

OC-022 repeats the OC-020 graded racetrack with a second spec bound to a
physically different conductor: `robinson-shanghai-hflt-v3` — Shanghai
Superconductor HFLT (sample SHS112 / SC8631244-3-2, DOI
10.6084/m9.figshare.5331145.v3, CC-BY-4.0), measured on the same Robinson
instrument at the same criterion over the identical declared
characterization window (20–40 K, 1–8 T, 0–180°). 1,504 selected points,
attested and registry-countersigned like the AP dataset. The specimen is a
0.5 mm bridge patterned from a 4 mm tape (vs AP's 1 mm bridge from 12 mm) —
per-width capacity is compared at the case's 12 mm tape width and the width
difference is a stated limitation, not modeled.

## The measured difference

At 30 K the two tapes' per-width `Ic` diverge with field in the direction a
graded design cares about: near-comparable at 1 T (SH ≈ AP within ~2% at
θ=0°), with the SH advantage widening as field rises — at 8 T, SH carries
~1.3× AP's per-width current in the worst-case orientation. Inner turns of a
bore-field winding sit at the pack's highest field; that is exactly where a
premium tape should pay.

## What calibration surfaced

Three real findings, all now in the record:

1. **The clamp is per-dataset, not per-policy.** The spec initially copied
   OC-020's `low_field_clamp_t: 0.0501` — correct for the lowfield dataset
   (floor 0.05 T), wrong for SH (floor 1.0 T). Sub-1 T queries retried at the
   clamp fell outside the mesh → `unsupported` → every SH-inner candidate
   INCONCLUSIVE. Correct value: `1.001` — just above the measured floor, the
   same clamp the base binding declares.

2. **Real data trips the monotonicity audit.** The SH CSV has 4 of 1,289
   nominal `(T, θ)` field-sweep pairs where measured `Ic` *increases* from
   7 → 8 T, worst +2.32% — plausibly IV-fit noise at the instrument's field
   limit, but real non-monotonicity. Contract A8 is unforgiving here: a
   failed audit sends every lower-bound point to `unsupported`. The binding
   declares `monotonicity_tolerance: 0.025` — justified against the audit,
   following OC-006's tolerance-justification pattern; the base binding keeps
   0.001. This is what dataset-specific declared policy is *for*.

3. **The coarse turn plan missed an interior lobe again.** The refined
   plan's worst point sat at ~0.85·N (turn 170 of 200 at `s15`) that the
   {2, 0.125N, 0.5N, N} coarse plan skipped — 3.9% shortfall → acceptance
   FAIL. A 0.85-fraction index fixed it (shortfall ≤ 0.21%).

## Honest limits

- **Prices are invented placeholders** (AP $30/m, SH $34/m — a ~13% premium
  for the stronger high-field tape). The 2.68% figure demonstrates the
  selection machinery on measured data; it is not a market claim and is
  sensitive to the declared premium by construction.
- Both datasets are 0.5–1 mm patterned bridges normalized per width;
  comparing them at 12 mm is a per-width capacity comparison, not a claim
  about purchasable full-width products (SH is sold as a 4 mm class tape).
- Still screening-fidelity: no strain model, no quench, no cable
  architectures, tape width is case-global.
