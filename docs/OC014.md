# OC-014: self-field-corrected screening — the tape-edge boundary

Status: PHASE 2 IMPLEMENTED (`critical_state_strip`, strict edge-field
bound; phase-1 `uniform_transport` retained unchanged). Motivated by
OC-010: every thin-pack candidate in the small-R corner passed
requirement and geometry but went INCONCLUSIVE on the
`max_self_field_ratio` gate (worst seen 5.16 vs declared 0.1). The
entire cheaper-than-parity design region sits behind this gate.

## What the gate actually measures

`self_field_ratio = μ0·K_used / (2·|B_query|)` — for a thin strip
carrying *uniform* sheet current K (A/m), the self-field at the strip's
own surface is exactly `μ0K/2` (sheet jump condition; the perpendicular
component vanishes on the strip plane by symmetry). The ratio is
literally "the tape's own transport self-field divided by the applied
field it was screened against."

When the ratio exceeds the declared limit, the applied-field Ic query
understates the field the tape sits in — the current verdict
(INCONCLUSIVE) is the honest "we cannot vouch" answer. The question
OC-014 asks: can we compute the *total* field well enough to turn some
of those into real verdicts?

## The physics, honestly stated

Two regimes, different models:

**Ratio ≲ 1 — uniform-transport bound (implementable now).**
Corrected query field: `B_query' = |B_applied| + μ0·K_tape/2` where
`K_tape = current_a / (tape_width_m · strands_parallel)` — the carried
current, not the capacity. This is an upper bound on the total field
magnitude (self-field adds at worst in the applied direction; higher
query field → lower queried Ic → conservative). Under it, a candidate
that passes is defensible; the residual caveat is the *angle* of the
self-field (in-plane tangential — using the applied angle is a
simplification to declare).

**Ratio ≳ 1 — critical state (not the first-order fix).**
When self-field dominates, the strip's current redistributes (Bean /
Norris 1970 thin strip): current crowds the edges, the edge field is
formally 1/√x singular in the zero-thickness limit, and the uniform-K
bound *understates* the edge field — no longer conservative. Correct
answers require either a critical-state strip model (Norris transport
solution / Brandt current density) or a finite-thickness standoff
evaluation. Under the first-order model, ratio > limit must still yield
INCONCLUSIVE — for a *correct* reason now documented, not silently.

Consequence for OC-010's open question: the ratio-5 cluster (40-turn
thin packs at R=0.024) stays INCONCLUSIVE under the first-order fix.
Those candidates need the critical-state model — Phase 2.

## Implementation shape (as built)

  `optcoil-coupled-search/v5` (model /v5, checker /v6).

  `current_a` (self-consistent — the self-field is produced by the
  current under test), add `μ0·K_tape/2` to the query magnitude before
  `query_mirror_pair_with_clamp`. Angle stays the applied angle
  (declared simplification: self-field is in-plane; we do not blend the

  `μ0·K_tape/(2·|B_applied|)` — over every evaluated point, not only
  the utilization-limiting one (dominance is worst where applied field

  `self_field_ratio` (k_used-based) stays a recorded diagnostic.

  acceptance rerun flows through the same `build_coupled_case` +
  `run_coupled_case` path, so the independent recompute applies the
  same model.

  the search case declares the correction (v1 forbids the field).
- The NumPy reference (`tools/reference_oc004.py`) applies the same
  bound and emits method `/v2`; `validate_reference` requires method
  ↔ case consistency exactly. `oc-004.reference.json` was regenerated
  (v1 case → all 90 points byte-identical; only the pinned

- The run record (`optcoil-coupled-run/v2`) reports
  `transport_self_field_ratio` per point and
  `max_transport_self_field_ratio` per candidate; under correction
  `query_field_t` *is* the corrected magnitude while `magnitude_t`
  keeps the applied one — both auditable per point.

## Phase 2 (the real answer for the small-R corner)

Critical-state strip model (Norris 1970 transport solution or a
Brandt-type current density) evaluated at the tape's finite
half-thickness standoff — the regime where current crowds the edges.
That is what actually unblocks the ratio-5 cluster.

### Measured residual population (the spec's target)

Now measured — and the measurement *changed* the spec. From the
complete OC-012 v5 grid (all 9 cells): the v4 `max_self_field_ratio`
gate was masking two different boundaries. With the Phase-1 correction
in place and the gate at the dominance boundary, every one of the 70
still-INCONCLUSIVE candidates decomposes as:

- **54 — dataset coverage** (53 only, 1 shared with the angle gate):
  the candidate's peak conductor field exceeds the measured dataset's
  8.0 T domain (thin packs at tight bends drive 9–17 T peak fields at
  arc apices). `unsupported` point counts > 0; the honest
  refusal-to-extrapolate working as designed. Unblocked by *data* (a
  dataset measured to ~10–17 T), not modeling.
- **17 — along-current field fraction > 0.20** (16 only, 1 shared):
  tape-edge points whose field component along the transport direction
  exceeds the limit the measured-Ic(θ) interpolation is defined over.
  Unblocked by an oblique-field material model — a different piece of
  physics than the critical-state profile entirely.
- **0 — true critical-state regime** (transport ratio > 1): in this
  study *no* candidate is held by the dominance boundary alone.

So Phase 2's value proposition narrows further than the pre-v5 estimate
suggested: the dominance-bound remnant may be small or absent at these
specs. The bigger levers are material-data coverage and oblique-field
modeling — *measurement/model-scope* items — and the OC-012 records now
say exactly which boundary holds each candidate. Phase 2 remains
necessary for correctness at specs whose feasible corridor crosses the
dominance boundary (higher currents, smaller bores), and for the
refined-plan edge regime below.

The OC-010 parity cell's refined-plan failure remains a Phase-2-relevant
case: `arc_60`, `width_index 4` (tape edge), refined capacity 1249.3 A
vs the coarse plan's 1252.0 A — the edge regime where both the
along-current fraction and the self-field profile matter.

### Empirical update from the OC-012 v6mx refined plan

The v6mx acceptance pass found the first concrete case where the
dominance boundary *does* bite — not at a tape edge but inside a
**field-null pocket** the coarse plan never samples. At the L=0.150
cells' best candidates, the refined 10-station plan (which adds every
5th turn) evaluates `straight_center`/junction-adjacent points on
interior tapes around turn ~135–145 where the applied field inside the
thick pack dips to ~0.03 T — a genuine interior flux-null region.
There `transport_self_field_ratio = μ0K/(2|B|)` reaches ~1.1 > 1, so
the dominance gate flips the candidate to INCONCLUSIVE even though
every sampled point individually PASSes (the clamped ~0.06 T query
yields ~4.4 kA allowed vs ~0.6 kA operating — the point is nowhere
near limiting).

So the measured "0 true critical-state candidates" above was a
*coarse-plan* artifact: the dominance boundary only reveals itself once
the refined plan samples into the interior null. This is the physically
correct trigger for Phase 2 — the null pocket is exactly where the
current is self-field-dominated and the uniform-transport sheet bound
is no longer a bound. The gate semantics are deliberately conservative
(it flags bound-validity, not danger — a 7×-margin null point still
sinks the candidate); whether a margin-aware gate is appropriate is a
contract decision, but the correct resolution is the critical-state
profile, not a relaxed flag.

### Model

1. Per evaluated point, solve the critical-state sheet-current profile
   J(x) across the tape width for applied field B_a and transport
   current I_t (Brandt–Indenbom thin-strip inversion / Norris transport
   solution). The uniform-K assumption is replaced by the actual
   critical-state profile: flux-free core, critical-current edges.
2. Evaluate the self-field from J(x) at a standoff equal to the tape
   half-thickness d/2 (the thin-strip edge field diverges
   logarithmically; the finite-thickness standoff is the physical
   regularization and matches how measured-Ic data is taken).
3. Query Ic at B_eff = |B_a + B_J(x)| and the corrected angle per point —
   the same interpolation path as Phase 1, only the queried field
   changes.
4. Replace the ratio > 1 INCONCLUSIVE gate with the physical verdict:
   I_t > Ic(B_eff, θ) is a FAIL; below is a PASS — *plus* an explicit
   model-uncertainty margin term until the strip model is validated
   against published strip cases (below). INCONCLUSIVE remains for
   genuine unsupported domains (interpolation bounds, missing data) —
   never silently rescinded.

### First-order bound derivation (why the current clamp is optimistic)

The null-pocket numbers are worth doing once by hand, because they
show the uniform-transport bound erring in the *unsafe* direction in
the dominance regime — not merely being inapplicable.

At the L=0.150 null pocket (`straight_center`, ~turn 135): applied
|B| ≈ 0.029 T, operating current ≈ 614 A over a 12 mm tape. The
uniform-transport query adds μ₀K/2 ≈ 0.06 T → queries at ~0.09 T,
clamped to the 0.05 T domain floor → Ic ≈ 4.4 kA, a 7× margin.

But the tape's *own* critical-state edge field is much larger than
μ₀K_op/2. For a thin strip (half-width w = 6 mm, REBCO thickness
d ≈ 1 µm), the Brandt–Indenbom edge-zone self-field at the flux front
scales as

    B_edge ~ (μ₀ K_c / 2π) · ln(4w / πd)

with K_c = Ic/(2w) the critical sheet current. Using Ic ≈ 4.4 kA →
K_c ≈ 367 kA/m, and ln(4w/πd) ≈ ln(0.024/3.1e-6) ≈ 9:

    B_edge ~ (4πe-7 · 367e3 / 2π) · 9 ≈ 0.66 T.

So the true edge-region field is ~0.66 T, not 0.09 T — the
uniform-transport bound under-reads the local field by ~7× where it
matters. The capacity at 0.69 T (|B_app| + B_edge) is meaningfully
below 4.4 kA — plausibly ~2–3 kA — still above 614 A, but the margin
is ~4×, not 7×. The honest number needs the self-consistent solve:

    B_query = |B_app| + B_edge(Ic(B_query))      [fixed-point in Ic]

which converges because Ic(B) is monotone decreasing — start at
Ic⁰ = Ic(|B_app|), evaluate B_edge, re-query, iterate ~4–6 times.
The implemented Phase 2 should solve this, not the full J(x) — the
strip carries I_op iff the converged Ic(B_query) > I_op.

## As implemented (Phase 2, then Phase 2b)

**Phase 2 (records `/v2`, superseded):** the strict bound — because
Ic(B) is monotone decreasing, the low-field-floor critical sheet
current `K_c_floor` upper-bounds the critical sheet current at *every*
applied field the strip could see:

    B_edge ≤ (μ₀ K_c_floor / 2π) · ln(tape_width / layer_thickness)

with `K_c_floor` resolved over a near-perpendicular sector (the edge
self-field enters the broad face ~perpendicular to it).

**Phase 2b (method `/v3`, current):** the *field-consistent* bound —
the largest fixed point of

    B_edge = (μ₀/2π) · k_bound(|B_app| + B_edge) · ln(w / d)

where `k_bound(B)` is a per-case table over the dataset's own nominal
field levels: at each level, the largest Ic resolvable over *every*
measured angle level, then suffix-maxed so the table is a
non-increasing bounding function of field. Two subtleties make this a
bound rather than merely a fixed point: the all-angle max covers any
direction the edge-zone total field can take (an oblique applied field
rotates the edge-zone angle out of the perpendicular sector, where Ic
is higher — a sector-only table could underbound there), and the
suffix max upper-bounds the interpolated value between measured levels
even where the measured Ic(B) itself wiggles. The solve brackets
`[0, B_floor]` and returns the hi endpoint, which satisfies
`hi ≥ f(hi)` — still a bound, now evaluated at the field the edge zone
actually sees instead of the dataset floor.

The consequence: the bound is strictly tighter than the strict floor
everywhere the converged edge field sits above 0.05 T — most of all at
high applied fields, where the edge zone sustains far less than the
floor Ic — and is identical to it in the deepest nulls (where the
converged field stays near the floor). If nothing on the angle axis
resolves the table is `None` and every point is honestly INCONCLUSIVE —
never an uncorrected pass.

Each evaluated point queries the material at `|B_applied| + B_edge`;
because the bound is valid at any transport ratio, the ratio > 1
dominance demotion does not apply under this model — a null-pocket
point resolves to a determined PASS or FAIL instead of INCONCLUSIVE.
If the whole angle axis fails to resolve, the self-field is unbounded
and the point is honestly INCONCLUSIVE — never an uncorrected
applied-field pass.

Historical note on the floor resolution: the first Phase-2 records
(method `/v1`) evaluated `K_c_floor` at the applied-field angle —
numerically degenerate at a deep field null, where `atan2` of two
noise-dominated components folds toward 0°/180° and the modelext
coverage edge (~0.4°) left the floor query outside the hull →
`cs_unboundable` → INCONCLUSIVE clusters at the deepest nulls. Method
`/v2` resolved the floor over a near-perpendicular sector instead —
the edge self-field's physically relevant direction. Phase 2b's
all-angle table makes the question moot entirely: `k_bound` no longer
depends on the applied-field angle at all.
