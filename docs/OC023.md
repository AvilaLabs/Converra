# OC-023 — three-dataset graded pack: SuperPower AP + Shanghai HFLT + THEVA AP

**Status: implemented; first graded benchmark over three physically distinct
measured vendor datasets. `search_status INCONCLUSIVE` — the screening-passing
optimum is real, but the independent refined plan could not fully resolve it.
That outcome is the finding: the acceptance layer caught a measured-data
coverage boundary the coarse plan never sampled.**

## Why

OC-022 showed a graded pack beating the cheapest uniform-tape design on two
measured vendors. THEVA Pro-Line AP (`robinson-theva-ap-v2`, THV202 /
TPL4622-AP ID 230044, DOI 10.6084/m9.figshare.25893079.v2, CC-BY-4.0; same
Robinson instrument, same criterion, same declared window) adds a third,
stronger tape — 1,785 selected points, attested and registry-countersigned.
At 25 K the three tapes form a clean price-performance frontier (worst-angle
per-width Ic: THEVA > Shanghai > AP, the THEVA margin growing with field to
~1.6× AP at 8 T), which is exactly the structure a graded search should
exploit.

OC-023 keeps OC-022's requirement, geometry, sampling, limits and cost block;
the candidate grid is the subset around the OC-022 optimum (turns
200/240/300 × tapes 2/3) with each of the two half-turn regions choosing
among all three specs (9 assignments per geometry, 54 candidates) plus a
240×3 all-AP baseline. Prices are declared placeholders: AP $30/m,
Shanghai $34/m, THEVA $44/m.

## What the refined plan actually found

The acceptance's §9.4 refined plan (10 stations, every-5th-turn plus
near-face indices — 5,400 points vs the coarse 200) re-evaluated the best
candidate and returned **INCONCLUSIVE with zero shortfall**: the refined
minimum allowed current is identical to the coarse one (same limiting
point, station `a0` tape 2 turn 1), so no worse binding point exists — but
**two sampled points could not be resolved against the THEVA dataset at
all**.

The unsupported queries, pinned with `OPTCOIL_TRACE_UNSUPPORTED=1`:

```
station=s15 tape=1 turn=130 width=0  |B|=6.28 T  theta_raw=-179.951 deg
station=s15 tape=2 turn=130 width=4  |B|=6.28 T  theta_raw=-0.049 deg
```

Both are the same physical direction: field within ~0.05° of the tape
normal — the **fold seam** of the `period_180_field_reversal` angle
mapping. A query at θ≈179.95 retries at θ−180 ≈ −0.05°; under THEVA that
lands *just* outside the measured-coordinate hull, whose measured-angle
extremes near the operating point are **[−0.040°, 179.65°]** — the query
misses the hull by ~0.01°.

The same physical point resolves under SuperPower AP not because its data
is better, but because its Hall-angle measurement jitter is *larger*: its
measured hull extends to **−1.21° / 179.03°**, so the −0.05° retry is
interior. THEVA's cleaner angular measurements make its hull edge sit
~0.04° inside its own nominal box — a knife-edge the coarse plan's five
turn indices happened never to query, and the refined plan's 54 did.

## Why this is reported, not patched

The contract's measured-coordinate interpolation deliberately never
extrapolates past the measured hull; a ~0.01° knife-edge miss is the honest
cost of that rule, and the fail-closed INCONCLUSIVE is the correct verdict.
The fix path is a *declared* modeling assumption — e.g. an angular-seam
stitching contract that wraps measured θ across the 0/180 fold using the
dataset's own measured periodicity, or a vendor dataset with quantified
angular tolerance — not a silent tolerance bump. Neither was in this record;
the stitching contract has since been implemented — see below.

This is also the strongest in-repo demonstration yet of why the evidence
layer exists: a screening-only pipeline would have shipped a 16.2% savings
claim on a design whose mid-pack tape edges sit at a measured-data boundary.

## Resolution: the declared seam contract (implemented)

The prescribed fix path — a declared modeling assumption, not a tolerance
bump — is now implemented as `angle_mapping:
period_180_field_reversal_seam_stitched` on the material binding. When a
binding declares it, the interpolator builds, for every complete nominal
(T, B) cell column, seam tetrahedra joining the last nominal angle level's
measured points to the first level's (the first level's measured angle
shifted +180°). A seam estimate is a convex blend of measured points on
*both* sides of the fold — interpolation across the declared period, never
extrapolation past the measured hull. A nonpositive measured gap, a hole at
either extreme level, or a span wider than `limits.angle_deg` produces no
cell (counted in the mesh summary), and field/temperature coverage still
gates — a query outside the measured (T, B) hull stays unsupported in every
representation.

`benchmarks/coupled/oc-023-seam.json` is this case with the declaration on
the `hts-theva` binding only; `runs/oc-023-seam.json` records the result:

- the THEVA dataset builds **24 seam cells** (all (T, B) columns complete,
  zero span rejections); the Shanghai binding, still declaring the plain
  mapping, builds none — the assumption is per-binding and visible verbatim
  in the record's case JSON.
- the previously-unsupported fold queries resolve, so the cheapest
  screening-PASS candidate now survives the §9.4 refined plan directly —
  **240×2 graded at $50,489.97, 16.23% modeled savings**, `search_status
  PASS`, `acceptance agreement PASS`, no fallback walk required (the same
  nominal figure this record originally reported INCONCLUSIVE).

What this does **not** claim: the seam contract is the declared statement
that THEVA's measured values are the same physical quantities one angular
period apart — it is not a vendor angular-tolerance measurement, not
manufacturing coverage, and not a claim that the operating point is
qualified. `conductor_qualification_status` remains INCONCLUSIVE and
`engineering_status` NOT_EVALUATED. A vendor dataset with quantified
angular tolerance remains the stronger evidence path; the declaration
exists precisely so that if such data arrives and contradicts the periodic
assumption, the record shows which assumption to retract.
