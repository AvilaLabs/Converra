# OC-026 — SPARC CSMC public-record reconstruction (solenoid class)

`benchmarks/coupled/oc-026-csmc.json` · record written to `runs/` (ignored)

## Purpose

OC-025 reconstructed the TFMC — a D-shaped pancake magnet. OC-026 adds a
second, independent public anchor on a different geometry class: the
**SPARC Central Solenoid Model Coil (CSMC)**, a layer-wound circular
solenoid of PIT-VIPER cable, tested by CFS/MIT in the same facility in
Aug–Oct 2024. Where TFMC screened at ~2% utilization, the CSMC winding is
genuinely loaded — the screening verdict here has real content.

## Reference data (published)

- CFS press release 2024-11-18: **50 kA** operating current, **5.7 T**
  field, 20 K, ~4 T/s ramps, quench-tested.
- Baldini/Marchevsky ASC-2024 slides: **4 nested layers × 10 turns**,
  **1.2 m inner diameter**, stored energy 3.728 MJ, 4 quench-detection
  fibers (1 per petal).
- Sanabria et al., SUST 37 (2024) 115010: PIT-VIPER cable — 4 partitioned
  copper petals each holding a REBCO tape stack; 23–25 mm cable OD class.
- IAEA FEC 2025 overview: first layer independently tested to 43.3 kA.

## Model mapping

- Circular path: single 360° arc at R = 0.648 m (1.2 m ID + half the
  ~96 mm radial build of 4 layers at 24 mm pitch).
- `turns_along_normal = 4` (radial layers), `tapes_along_width = 10`
  (axial cable turns), `tape_width_m`/`radial_pitch_m` = 24 mm (cable OD
  midpoint of the published 23–25 mm range).
- `strands_parallel` is **not published** — the petal stack count is
  proprietary. Declared as a sweep over 100/140/180 tapes per cable
  (25–45 per petal): the result is a *conditional certificate* across the
  uncertain parameter, not a fitted answer.
- Requirement: declared 2.0 T bore-center target — the probe sits at the
  solenoid center. The published "5.7 T" is not labeled bore or
  peak-on-conductor in the public record; see Findings.
- Dataset `robinson-superpower-ap-v3-modelext` (measured + declared model
  extension); CSMC's tape suppliers are not public — the case certifies
  the winding, not a vendor choice. 25 K declared (the coil runs 20 K).

## Results

All three strand-count candidates: requirement PASS, screening PASS,
refinement PASS, independent acceptance **agreement PASS** (sampling
shortfall ~9e-9).

- NI solve for 2.0 T bore: NI = 2.097 MA·t → **I_op = 52.4 kA vs.
  published 50 kA (−5%)**.
- Modeled peak sampled field at that operating point: **4.88 T**.
- Utilization at the limiting point (tape 10, turn 1, width edge — the
  innermost layer's axial-end turn, where transverse field angle is
  largest): 26.7% / 19.0% / 14.8% for 100/140/180 strands — a physically
  sensible limiting location and a meaningfully loaded screen, unlike
  OC-025's ~2%.
- Peak transverse interface pressure ~19.6 MPa (recorded; no bound
  declared).

## Findings

1. **Second independent public anchor.** On a different magnet class
   (circular layer-wound solenoid vs. D-pancake), the as-built winding
   screens clean and the field model's implied operating current lands
   within ~5% of the published value — with zero fitted electrical
   parameters. Combined with OC-024 (17 T field parity, ~1%) and OC-025
   (TFMC screening + BOM consistency), the public-record validation set
   now spans two magnet families and three magnet programs.
2. **The published 5.7 T is a peak-on-conductor-class figure.** At the
   real 2.0 MA·t the model predicts 1.9 T bore-center — 5.7 T at the
   bore would require ~149 kA, three times the published current. The
   modeled 4.9 T peak sits within ~15% of the published figure,
   consistent with inferred winding pitch. This resolves the public
   record's ambiguity without touching it.
3. **The winding certifies across the unpublished stack count.** Every
   strands_parallel value in the plausible 100–180 range passes — the
   honest output under parameter uncertainty is "certified for any
   plausible stack count," which the choice sweep expresses directly.
4. **Robustness check on the low-field machinery.** Conductor fields run
   0.9–4.9 T — the sub-1 T interior points exercise the declared
   lower-bound policy, which the modelext dataset covers as estimates.

## Known limitations

- Tapes per petal stack, exact cable pitch, and axial height are
  inferred, not published — the strand sweep exists precisely because of
  this.
- The tape-normal orientation inside a PIT-VIPER petal is declared
  `radial`; the real cable's petal arrangement makes the true tape-face
  orientation azimuth-dependent.
- 25 K stands in for the real 20 K operating temperature.
- Prices are synthetic; the strands sweep's cost ordering is tape
  quantity, not procurement truth.
- This is a public-data reconstruction — not customer validation, not
  manufacturing qualification.
